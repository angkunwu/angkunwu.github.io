About

I’m a theoretical and computational physicist developing models and algorithms to understand complex, interacting systems.

I develop physics-informed neural architectures, generative representations, and inverse-modeling methods for scientific data. My work spans neural quantum states, Hamiltonian inference from scattering spectra, and strongly correlated quantum matter, connecting theoretical physics with statistical inference and GPU-accelerated computation.

My research has taken me to Los Alamos National Laboratory and the University of Tennessee, Knoxville. I earned my Ph.D. in Physics at Rutgers University, spent a pre-doctoral research term at the Flatiron Institute, and studied complex networks at Harvard Medical School. My undergraduate degree is from Zhejiang University’s Chu Kochen Honors College.

Experience

Apr 2025 — present

Postdoctoral Researcher

University of Tennessee, Knoxville

Advisors: Cristian D. Batista, Yang Zhang, and Adrian Del Maestro

Machine learning and optimization for strongly correlated electronic systems.

  • Showed that linear spin-wave theory (LSWT) could closely fit simulated interacting magnon peak energies while yielding the wrong sign for an exchange coupling. A network trained on LSWT spectra reproduced this error.
  • Benchmarked inference on LSWT spectra and interacting spectra from the truncated Hilbert-space ladder (THL) method. Conv1D performed best among the neural architectures tested on THL spectra, and Gaussian process regression led the classical methods tested on clean LSWT spectra. Used Fisher information to diagnose local identifiability and showed that selected polarization channels or out-of-plane momentum cuts resolved a separate coupling-sign ambiguity in the in-plane unpolarized THL spectra.
  • Improved robustness with Gaussian noise augmentation, studied Poisson counting noise for LSWT spectra, and trained Gaussian-output networks to estimate input-dependent conditional uncertainty. Built a learned forward decoder for rapid spectral reconstruction and self-consistency checks before full physical forward-solver validation; released SpinDynamicsML.
  • Designed a compact, physics-informed spin–charge-separated neural quantum-state architecture with fewer parameters than tested fully connected, convolutional, and transformer baselines. Demonstrated transfer of learned local rules to larger spin systems without retraining at the solvable valence-bond-solid (VBS) point. Released GPU-accelerated variational Monte Carlo software as NQSpy.
  • Proposed geometric mean pooling, a parameter-free signed pooling operator; proved preservation of a global multiplicative statistic under hierarchical pooling and evaluated synthetic, image, and molecular tasks. Released GeometricMeanPooling; investigating chiral superconductivity in moiré materials.

Los Alamos National Laboratory

Advisor: Shi-Zeng Lin

Strongly correlated topological systems and the geometry of interacting electrons.

Performed the numerical calculations for the Nature Communications study reporting signatures of a moiré Kondo insulator.
