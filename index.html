About

I’m a theoretical and computational physicist developing models and algorithms to understand complex, interacting systems.

I work on neural quantum states, inverse problems in neutron scattering, and strongly correlated electronic systems. My research connects theoretical physics with machine learning, statistical inference, and GPU-accelerated computation.

My research has taken me to Los Alamos National Laboratory and the University of Tennessee, Knoxville. I earned my Ph.D. in Physics at Rutgers University, spent a pre-doctoral research term at the Flatiron Institute, and studied complex networks at Harvard Medical School. My undergraduate degree is from Zhejiang University’s Chu Kochen Honors College.
